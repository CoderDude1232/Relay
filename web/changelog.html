Changelog

What's new

What's changed in the Relay app, month by month, newest first. The TestFlight beta is next — the download page has the latest.

October 2026

  • Sign in without a password. Get a one-time code by email instead. Forgotten passwords and email changes also work with emailed codes now.
  • Account password. Set one and it's required to sign in to your account on a new phone, so access to your email alone isn't enough to take it over.
  • Find Me by Email is opt-in. People can't find you by your email address unless you turn it on.
  • View-once media, forwarding and a QR scanner for adding people in person.
  • Polls show their question in the chat and the chat list, and support multiple choices. Polls, locations and notes now show the time and delivery status.
  • Location messages show just the map, with a full-screen live map when you tap it.
  • Photo-only and emoji-only messages show their time and delivery status underneath.
  • Photos have their location and other metadata removed before they're sent.
  • Links you receive ask before opening, and map previews don't load until you ask.
  • Incoming call alerts no longer carry who's calling; your phone learns that from the encrypted call itself.
  • New calls move to a backup call relay when ours is down or overloaded, so calls keep connecting.
  • Linking a device and Emergency Wipe now ask for your device passcode.
  • Your devices list shows each device's model and region, shared end-to-end encrypted, and no longer shows duplicates.
  • Report and block. Report a contact, a group, or a member of a group from their info screen.
  • Delete your account from your profile. Deleting it also revokes Sign in with Apple.
  • Your contacts are only checked when you tap Find Contacts, and Relay remembers your answer.
  • iCloud backups run daily, stored under a name that can't be tied to your Relay account.
  • On iPad: easier-to-read message widths, and ⌘↩ to send.
  • Clearer error messages, VoiceOver labels on icon-only buttons and the safety-number screen, and a refreshed welcome screen.

September 2026

  • Sealed sender on every message: our servers can't see who sent it.
  • Blinded contact discovery. The usernames and emails you look up are scrambled on your phone, so our servers never see them.
  • Encrypted groups. Group names, members and admins are kept by the members' phones only; our servers keep no group records. Group invite links are encrypted too.
  • Encrypted iCloud Backup, protected by a recovery key only you hold.
  • Multi-device sync. What you do on one of your devices is mirrored to the others.
  • Voice and video calls, relayed through our call server by default so the other person never sees your IP address.
  • Sign in with Apple or Google, plus contact invite links and a QR code to share.
  • Notifications are a content-free wake-up by default. Faster Alerts, if you turn it on, carries the encrypted message inside the push.
  • Our servers stop storing your email address in readable form.
  • Account recovery, group profiles and username search.
  • Live location updates as one moving bubble instead of a new message each time.
  • Messages keep arriving when the usual connection is blocked.
  • Reactions and a native press-and-hold menu on messages, and a new app icon.

August 2026

  • Group chats, end-to-end encrypted.
  • Voice notes and file attachments, with previews in the composer.
  • Profiles. Your display name and photo, shared end-to-end encrypted with the people you talk to.
  • Contact cards and custom names for your contacts.
  • Custom chat wallpapers and bubble colours.

July 2026

  • The first build: one-to-one messaging, end-to-end encrypted with the Signal Protocol and a post-quantum key exchange.
  • Safety numbers, to check no one is sitting between you and the person you're talking to.
  • Photos, encrypted on your phone before they're sent.
  • Push notifications.
  • Unsend a message you've sent.
